LC-0012 open added 2026-10-27

On gamma-TiAl the 1969 fluoride-inhibits-hot-salt-SCC result may invert — because the surface fluoride that strips as volatile TiF4 at ~284 C on low-Al titanium shifts toward non-volatile AlF3 on a 45-50 at% Al alloy at 300-500 C — so the "fluorine effect" patented and in service for TiAl oxidation (700-1050 C, unstressed) may actually sensitise the same component under NaCl in the stressed 300-500 C regime it has never been tested in.

Old source
R. S. Ondrejcin, "Chlorine Gas and Fluoride Ion in Hot-Salt Stress Corrosion Cracking of Titanium-Aluminum Alloys," DP(NASA)-1179, AEC R&D Report, Savannah River Laboratory, E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co., Aiken SC, March 1969 (NASA Purchase Order R-124). Section "Fluoride Ion in Hot-Salt Stress Corrosion Cracking," Tables II-IV, Figures 2-3. https://sti.srs.gov/fulltext/DP(NASA)-1179.pdf — verifiers OCR'd the report in full; Table IV tritium counts (NaF 0 c/min after washing, no cracking; NaCl 244, NaBr 110, NaI 239, all cracked) and the "Absorption of High-Purity Hydrogen Gas" counter-result (NaF-treated Ti-5Al-2.5Sn gained 6.6 mg vs 3.0 mg blank at 500 C and fractured more severely at 330 C) both verified verbatim.
Modern problem
The modern "fluorine effect" / halogen-effect literature on gamma-TiAl (HF dip, F implantation, polymer spray) is validated only for unstressed oxidation at 700-1050 C with mass-gain / scale-constitution endpoints; the fluorine-effect review (IntechOpen ch. 61032) does not mention NaCl, sea salt, hot-salt corrosion, SCC or hydrogen embrittlement, and its thermodynamics run 700-1100 C with the lowest actual test at 900 C. Patented in service for turbocharger rotors. Nothing in the modern corpus tests 300-500 C because below ~600 C oxidation is not the design concern — so the regime where HSSCC happens is precisely the regime the oxidation community has no reason to visit.
Cheapest next check
Before any bench spending, send one email to Mathias Galetz / Alexander Donchev at DECHEMA (custodians of the fluorine effect) asking whether any chloride or salt-deposit testing of F-treated TiAl exists in unindexed proceedings or licensee qualification reports — the cheapest possible refutation of the novelty claim, ~$0. If that doesn't close it: 12 Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb coupons, four-arm triplicate (polished±NaCl, HF-dipped±NaCl), 100 h at 400 C in moist air; endpoint is bulk H by vacuum hot extraction (LECO RHEN, ~$40/coupon) plus SEM crack census on tensile face; pre-registered rule — arm 2 (fluorinated+NaCl) worse than arm 1 means fluorination sensitises, equal or better means the 1969 inhibition transfers. ~$5-8k, 6-8 weeks, one graduate student.

LC-0013 open added 2026-10-27

Transfer the photographic industry's closed-loop free-halide-activity control (constant pAg/pBr double-jet precipitation, with ion activity as the regulated variable) to polar antisolvent/LARP crystallization of CsPbX3 perovskite nanocrystals as a reproducibility instrument — a field that currently regulates nominal precursor molar ratios and closes its loops on photoluminescence has never measured the free halide activity that actually sets the nucleation-and-growth driving force.

I. du Pont de Nemours and Co., filed 1975-02-28 (CIP of a 1972-08-14 application), granted 1979-04-03; read alongside N. Calamur, "Jet mixing in preparation of monodisperse silver halide emulsions," US 4,251,627, DuPont, filed 1978-05-30, granted 1981-02-17. https://patents.google.com/patent/US4147551A/en — both patents fetched and body-verified by two verifiers; pAg meter + control signal quotes, 6-11 range, 6.4 and 7.1-7.3 examples, Example 5 GSD 1.27 / skew -0.079 invention vs GSD 1.69 control, and US 4,251,627's "carefully controlled constant pAg environment" with stepped setpoints (8.5→9.0, 8.5→9.5, 5.1→6.7) all confirmed verbatim.
Modern problem
Modern halide perovskite nanocrystal synthesis (LARP and hot-injection CsPbX3) regulates nominal Br/Pb precursor stoichiometry and uses photoluminescence (PLQY, linewidth) as its in-situ handle (e.g. F. Bateni 2023 NC State PhD thesis; AIChE 2024/2025 proceedings from the same group). Europe PMC co-occurrence (verified by both surviving verifiers): "controlled double jet" AND perovskite = 0; "halide ion-selective electrode" AND nanocrystal = 0; "bromide ion-selective electrode" AND perovskite = 0; "habit modifier" AND "halide perovskite" = 0. No perovskite platform measures free halide activity. A documented corporate discontinuity (AgfaPhoto insolvency 2005; Kodak Ch.11 2012 with Research Labs dismantled) left the photographic precipitation physical chemistry with no academic successor community, and the content lives in a patent estate not indexed in abstract databases perovskite groups search.
Cheapest next check
Establish whether a halide ion-selective electrode gives a usable Nernstian response in the actual LARP solvent before anything else. One solid-state Ag2S/AgBr combination ISE (Metrohm 6.0502.180 or Thermo Orion 9435, ~$350-500) plus double-junction reference with non-aqueous-compatible bridge (~$250), on a benchtop pH/mV meter most labs own. Titrate TOABr or CsBr stock into the real antisolvent mix (toluene/DMF or hexane/octylamine-oleic acid as used for CsPbBr3 LARP) over 4-5 decades of free Br and plot mV vs log[Br]. Pass criterion: slope within 10% of 59 mV/decade, drift under 2 mV over 10 min. Half a day, under $1000 of hardware, one person. If non-Nernstian or the junction fouls, the entire lead dies there.

The refuting verifier (score 3) raised a mechanism/physics objection worth carrying: halide ISEs are aqueous devices with reportedly non-Nernstian Br response in aprotic solvents, in DMF/DMSO bromide is complexed as PbBr(3-/4-) so free-halide activity is not an independent driving force, and the timescales are inverted (double-jet runs 15-60 min; LARP nucleation is mixing-limited and over in <~200 ms). Other carried caveats: Nature Synthesis 2024 (PMC11466857) concludes that "relying on supersaturation-growth rate relationship for indirect control of MAPbBr3 growth is inaccurate" in bulk evaporative single-crystal growth — the strongest extant argument against the driving-force handle, unrebutted. Neither cited patent gives an electrode type, tolerance band, or coefficient of variation. Scout confidence 0.4 (unusually low, honestly reported).

LC-0006 open added 2026-10-22

Gamma-TiAl may have a hot-salt stress-corrosion-cracking (HSSCC) onset temperature below the ~260-290 C "floor" assumed from the 1962-72 Ti-alloy corpus, because the alumina scale that Ondrejcin (NASA CR-1133, 1968) identified as a catalytic accelerator of NaCl pyrohydrolysis is the native scale of a 48 at% Al aluminide from t=0, maximising the HCl source term the mechanism needs.

Old source
Ondrejcin, R. S., and Louthan, M. R. Jr., "Role of Hydrogen Chloride in Hot-Salt Stress Corrosion Cracking of Titanium-Aluminum Alloys," NASA CR-1133 (also DP(NASA)-1130), E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co., Savannah River Laboratory, August 1968. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19680023547/downloads/19680023547.pdf — body, selective-attack table (Table II: oxide/metal Al ratios 2-3x on Ti-Al alloys), and p.17 "aluminium oxide acts as an accelerator in the pyrohydrolysis of difficultly hydrolyzable salts such as NaCl." Accelerator claim cites Kirk-Othmer Encyclopedia of Chemical Technology v.14 p.215 (1955) and Rodden ed., Analytical Chemistry of the Manhattan Project p.729 (McGraw-Hill 1950) — neither retrieved; this is the lead's weakest joint.
Modern problem
Modern HSSCC literature (including Dye-group work, e.g. arXiv:2009.10567 and D2-18O crack-tip experiments at 450 C) inherits Ondrejcin's conclusion (susceptibility rises with Al; Al is preferentially attacked) while dropping his accelerator mechanism. The 1962-72 stressed test matrix capped at ~8 wt% Al (Ti-8Al-1Mo-1V the high-Al extreme); the TiAl-era literature uses an unstressed superalloy hot-corrosion protocol at 700-900 C. No source puts stressed bulk gamma-TiAl under solid NaCl in 300-500 C. The two literatures describe the same quantity (Al2O3 at a chloride interface) with opposite valence, and nothing forces them to meet.
Cheapest next check
Two paper + bench steps BEFORE any SCC rig. (a) Interlibrary-loan Kirk-Othmer 1955 v.14 p.215 and Rodden 1950 p.729; verify whether either names Al2O3 (vs U3O8/WO3/V2O5) as a pyrohydrolysis accelerator. ~$0-40, 3-10 days. (b) HCl-evolution screen on 0.1 mg/cm2 solid NaCl over (i) alpha-Al2O3 powder, (ii) TiO2 powder, (iii) 50/50 mix, (iv) scraped Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb scale, (v) blank — isothermal holds at 400/350/300/250/200 C in moist air, chloride by IC on NaOH-trapped effluent. ~$4-8k, 2-3 weeks. Only if alumina raises chloride yield over TiO2 in 250-350 C should the stress ladder be built.

Verifier notes

Verifiers in the upstream ledger independently fetched both primary copies of CR-1133 (NTRS and sti.srs.gov DP(NASA)-1130), extracted the text locally, and confirmed the accelerator sentence verbatim on p.17, along with Table II's Al enrichment numbers (28.6/7.7, 7.8/5.9, 13.9/5.2, 11.6/4.3), the V/Sn-depletion statement that makes the enrichment Al-specific, and the NaOH-strips-Al mitigation control. One verifier scored 7 and called this "the strongest-sourced lead I have checked in this series." The author line and report numbers were read directly from the SRS title page, not reconstructed from a metadata aggregator.

Where the lead is exposed is not citation fidelity but the depth of the citation chain beneath Ondrejcin's own accelerator sentence: it rests on a 1955 handbook cite with no measurement, and the modern pyrohydrolysis-accelerator literature names WO3, U3O8 and Cr2O3 while treating alumina as the matrix, never the catalyst. The bridge also quietly converts "composition of a reactively-grown corrosion product" into "Al2O3 activity in the protective scale", which are different physical objects.

The one upstream verifier who voted refuted (score 3) argued: CR-1133 Table III reports a Ti-8Al-1Mo-1V hot-salt crack threshold temperature of 232 C — on the alloy with the 28.6 wt% Al corrosion product, i.e. the maximum-scale-alumina case in the 1962-72 matrix. A ~4x spread in scale Al across that series moved severity and threshold stress, never onset temperature; lower-Al Ti-6Al-4V has a reported field failure at 220 C (wrong sign); and at 300-500 C a TiAl scale is a thin transient mixed TiO2/Al2O3 film, not protective alpha-alumina — so interfacial alumina activity may be far lower than the bulk composition suggests. The two paper + bench steps above are designed to settle the catalytic-rationale half directly before anyone commits to an SCC rig.

Caveats carried from the upstream verifiers:

- The Cranfield PhD thesis "Elevated Temperature Oxidation and Corrosion of a Titanium Aluminide Alloy" (handle 1826/3584) is the closest grey-literature threat. Indirect reporting indicates air/SO2 kinetics at or above 800 C (not NaCl, not stressed, not the lead's window), but the document itself could not be opened (HTTP 403) and should be pulled from the Cranfield repository before anyone commits to the stress ladder. - Yao & Marek (1995) found no NaCl-induced corrosion of TiAl in oxygen-free exposure, so the route needs oxygen/moisture — consistent with pyrohydrolysis. - Dye's group argues oxygen (not hydrogen) from water vapour is the main embrittling species at 450 C, which would partly decouple cracking severity from the HCl source term this lead keys on. - ONERA's US 8,617,323 B2 already treats salt-exposed TiAl parts as needing protection and describes an AlCl3 → HCl → re-attack loop; the chemistry is not virgin ground even if the stressed threshold question is.

Verifier notes

The upstream ledger entry survived its three verifiers with no refuted votes (scores 8/5/5). All three fetched Wiederhorn & Bolz (one via r.jina.ai after finding the belglas scan was CCITT image-only, one by parsing the PDF content streams directly) and confirmed Table II's 18 values with standard deviations, Eqs. 1-5, the distilled-water 2-90 C methods, the static-fatigue-limit asymmetry statement, and the abstract's "Sodium was detrimental to stress corrosion resistance." A sibling entry (upstream score 8/3/7, same date) argues the same bridge from the threshold-asymmetry angle — that for a permanently loaded part a threshold is the only thing that buys unbounded life, and the alkali-free chemistries selected for glass cores are the two in which W&B observed no threshold over their measured range. Both entries recommend the same dynamic-fatigue measurement; this lead merges them.

Caveats the upstream verifiers raised, carried verbatim:

1. **The form is exponential (Charles-Hillig), not the power law v = A K^n the modern packaging literature uses.** NASA's own TM-103543 states that at a proof ratio of 3.0 the two forms differ by a factor of ~60 in predicted life for soda-lime — the output is model-form-sensitive before it is n-sensitive. The deliverable is a bound family, not a lifetime number.

2. **"Aluminosilicate I" is low-alkali (Na2O 0.01 wt fraction), NOT alkali-free, and is a 1723-type glass, not AF32 or Eagle XG.** The paper's own headline finding is that sodium content dominates stress-corrosion resistance, which is the axis a substitution needs to clear.

3. **W&B themselves state the universal-fatigue-curve equation "does not describe stress corrosion phenomena for very short failure times or for failure times approaching the fatigue limit"**, and that the curves are valid only to log t ~ 3.5. A glass-core 10-year service life is exactly the long-duration extrapolation they disclaim.

4. **The no-threshold result for aluminosilicate is an absence of evidence** the authors explicitly disown ("although one presumably exists for aluminosilicate and silica glasses at lower stress intensity values"). The sharpened claim is "the no-growth stress is unmeasured for this chemistry," not "alkali-free glass has no threshold." Grutzik et al. 2022 and later work partly fill the threshold question in for silica; the alkali-free aluminosilicate chemistries of AF32/Eagle XG remain unmeasured at 25 C.

5. **The measured and calculated characteristic durations do not agree quantitatively in the source itself** (Section IV.3): the experimental slope is ~3x the calculated one, attributed to real abrasion flaws being irregular. This is directly relevant to a laser-ablated plus HF-etched TGV sidewall and is the citation for why a single predicted lifetime would be unjustified.

6. **The claim that the connection is "wholly unmade" was damaged by one verifier**: McCann's 2017 Georgia Tech dissertation does cite W&B 1970 (as [86]) along with Charles's two Static Fatigue of Glass papers, Gy 2003, Ciccotti 2009 and Glaesemann's fibre-reliability method, and reproduces a v-vs-K_I figure. What survives is narrower: packaging stops at a moisture-reduced snapshot threshold and does not integrate v(K_I) forward to a service-life bound, and a region-0 threshold for the specific alkali-free aluminosilicate core chemistries at 25 C does appear unmeasured.

7. **Correction to upstream numbers**: Aluminosilicate I's b is 0.138 (not 0.144); the paper names fused silica as the most stress-corrosion-resistant glass, not aluminosilicate, and groups Aluminosilicate I with borosilicate as intermediate. The counterintuitive direction survives in narrower form.
