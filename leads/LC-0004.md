All three source-side verifiers independently fetched Sparrow et al. 1994 and confirmed the paraphrase sentence by sentence: six matched-rate Russet Burbank field experiments, banded DAP plus either K2SO4 or KCl; at 4 of 6 sites tuber AND petiole Cd were 20-30% lower under K2SO4; the authors call it of "considerable promise". The companion 1993 paper's null result on broadcast lime across pH 5.2-7.1 on high-OM krasnozems is a direct warning for high-OM cacao soils, where lime trials have been slow and inconsistent.

The cacao-side omission was checked against the AUTHORITATIVE documents (PNS BAFS 421:2025 and the Alliance Bioversity-CIAT / NICOLE mitigation report), not asserted. A Colombian greenhouse study reports a soil-K / foliar-Cd correlation (r2 = -0.56) with no reference to the accompanying anion — the field has the data but not the concept.

Caveats raised in the source ledger, all preserved verbatim:

1. **Not novel as a concept, only as a transfer.** CSIRO's own potato grower brochure and an Australian fertiliser-industry agritopic already advise sulphate over muriate of potash to manage Cd. The novelty is strictly that nobody has run it in cacao.
2. **Effect size is 20-30%, below the 30-60% the problem needs**, and it is a tuber/petiole result, not a seed result.
3. **The sulphate half of the mechanism is weaker than the chloride half, and the scout conflated them.** Wang et al. 2019 found K2SO4 slightly INCREASED Cd in wheat and pak choi and reduced it substantially only in rice, where sulphate reduction to sulphide and CdS precipitation operates — a mechanism that cannot run in aerobic cacao soil. Hussain et al. 2022 found sulphate had no significant field effect on rice grain Cd. The aerobic prior rests essentially on the one 1994 potato series.
4. **The failure mode predicts attenuation in the target environment.** Sparrow's two null sites were attributed to leaching and background irrigation chloride. LAC cacao sits in 1800-3000 mm/yr humid tropics where chloride is the most leachable anion, so fertiliser Cl may be a transient pulse rather than a standing condition. Read the other way, this says the effect appears only where fertiliser Cl dominates the Cl budget. **This is the single biggest hole.**
5. Much LAC cacao is low-input; on many exceeding farms there is no KCl to substitute. K2SO4 also costs well above KCl per unit K at cacao K rates of 118-176 kg K/ha, so "no cost increase of consequence" is unsupported.
6. No chlorine requirement or Cl toxicity threshold for Theobroma cacao was located; the agronomic-risk claim is unevaluated.
