Caveats carried from the verifiers, in strict form:

1. **The naive three-way-ratio inversion the first draft proposed does not work.** Landauer's Eq. 12 angular factor gives a vertical/oblique ratio bounded on (1, 1.657]; the measured ratio is 3.3-3.5, outside the admissible range for every compressibility, and the horizontal/oblique prediction is strictly positive while the measurement is negative. Landauer himself attributes the anisotropy to a 40x viscosity gradient across the overburden, not to compressibility. The reframed check (oblique diameters + deep-pit depth-dependence against a depth-resolved viscosity field) survives, which is the version above.

2. **Table VII's m(z) column is imported from SIPRE Report 7 lab tests, not measured by this field experiment.** The "field-anchored Poisson curve" framing is incorrect; what is field-measured are the strain rates, densities and calculated overburden stresses.

3. **Landauer's own theory under-predicts the field closure substantially** (theoretical vs observed radial strain rates 14 vs 35 x1e-10 s^-1 vertically, 2 vs -0.9 horizontally; deep-pit observed rates 1.5-2.6x the calculated ones). He attributes this to using viscosity at the tunnel centre when the softer overlying firn controls roof descent; the same direction (real firn softer under deviatoric load than Sorge-law viscosity implies) is why the 2025 paper needed a large k.

4. **Landauer assumes a linear Newtonian flow law; GM97 uses n = 3.** His fitted parameters cannot be transplanted; only his raw closure/strain-rate data can be re-inverted.

5. **Bender 1967 (Sapporo Physics of Snow and Ice proceedings) re-tested the same inversion on the same tunnel after 11 years and reported failure, with the same failure at Wilkes (Cameron 1961) and South Pole (Ramseier 1962).** Any writeup should anchor on Bender rather than Landauer, and should include a vertical-only background densification correction (worth 15-25% of the vertical closure per Bender).

6. **The "corpus-unreachable" story is softer than first claimed.** The 2025 paper does cite CRREL TR 174 (Clark 1965, Camp Century) and the 2026 preprint cites CRREL RR 220, Bader, Salm and Shinojima — the group is fluent in this literature and simply prefers lab calibration.
