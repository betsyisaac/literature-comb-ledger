The two damaging verifiers raised issues that must travel with the lead:

1. **The Lusatian corpus is partly reachable in English already** (Kudla, Rosenzweig, Szczyrba & Weissbach 2022, 20th ICSMGE Sydney, publishes the 125 events, 0.01 ha to 1.79 km^2, 0-8 degrees, in the ISSMGE open library; Kudla et al. 2015, XVI ECSMGE). What is not transposed is the arrest criterion, not the dataset.

2. **The predictor needs pre-failure terrain and phreatic surface, which Ghahramani's 33 cases (selected on post-event imagery alone) do not have — so h_F/h_K is not computable for them.** The proposed cheap check cannot be run against the Ghahramani set as written; it has to be run against the smaller class of flow failures (listed above) that DO have published pre-failure state. Verifier 3 refuted the lead on this ground.

3. **Different dependent variable.** Wittig's criterion predicts whether horizontal flow initiates and where it arrests, not how far material travels. Ghahramani's confined/unconfined variable is a lateral-spreading modifier on an area-volume regression among 33 events that all flowed. There is no no-flow class in Ghahramani to retrodict against; the honest reframing is "a pre-failure-terrain screening criterion calibrated on about 125 natural-trigger sand liquefactions, offered to a field that has 33 post-event-only cases," not "a better runout regression."

4. **Olson & Stark's yield / liquefied-strength ratios over ~30 back-analysed flow failures (including hydraulic fills and tailings) is a case-calibrated static-flow-failure screen that already exists** in which the driving stress ratio is central; this is directly at odds with Wittig's headline that slope angle drops out, which holds only in his zero-residual-strength limit.

5. **The German-venue gap mechanism is not the binding constraint** — the Dutch Zeeland/Scheldt flow-slide corpus is roughly an order of magnitude larger (>1000 events, ~200 analysed, pre/post bathymetry, published in English) and has also failed to reach the tailings area-volume literature. The pattern is a scope/discipline split, not a translation barrier.

6. **Scope limits of the mechanics itself.** Wittig assumes complete loss of shear strength beneath a planar seepage line with a free, mobilisable passive wedge in an earth-moist cap at the toe; a tailings impoundment with a constructed embankment, a supernatant pond and a downstream channel does not present that geometry. The mapping is to open hydraulic sand fills and upstream-raised sandy beaches, not a dam breach as such.

7. **Novelty gate.** Request the 2024 LBGR Brandenburg volume (Grosser & Szczyrba) by ILL before any writeup; if that volume already makes the tailings link the novelty claim is dead.
