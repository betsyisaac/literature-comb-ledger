The upstream verifier confirmed the Sugawara 1968 paper says what the scout claimed about membrane permeabilization and leakage of cell constituents in C. albicans, and located the supporting mid-century papers. The gap between the two modern reviews is real and was reproduced in search: a 2019 review of guanidine-containing polyhydroxyl macrolides tabulates Candida work for albicans and the classic non-albicans species only, and a 2022 review of guanidine antifungals discusses C. auris at length but contains no macrolides. No study pairs azalomycin F (or its congeners) with C. auris or the C. haemulonii complex.

Caveats to carry forward:

- Membrane-permeabilizing polyenes already treat C. auris (amphotericin B); the hypothesis is only interesting if azalomycin F has activity against amphotericin-B-resistant isolates, so the test panel must include them.
- Mid-century MICs on C. albicans do not automatically transfer to a different species in a different decade; "never tested" is a cheap-check argument, not a prediction of activity.
- Azalomycin F is a mixture of congeners historically; sourcing a reasonably pure preparation is part of the cost.
