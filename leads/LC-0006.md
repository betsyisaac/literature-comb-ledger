modern_problem: Modern HSSCC literature (including Dye-group work, e.g. arXiv:2009.10567 and D2-18O crack-tip experiments at 450 C) inherits Ondrejcin's conclusion (susceptibility rises with Al; Al is preferentially attacked) while dropping his accelerator mechanism. The 1962-72 stressed test matrix capped at ~8 wt% Al (Ti-8Al-1Mo-1V the high-Al extreme); the TiAl-era literature uses an unstressed superalloy hot-corrosion protocol at 700-900 C. No source puts stressed bulk gamma-TiAl under solid NaCl in 300-500 C. The two literatures describe the same quantity (Al2O3 at a chloride interface) with opposite valence, and nothing forces them to meet.

One verifier scored 7 and called this "the strongest-sourced lead I have checked in this series." The author line and report numbers were read directly from the SRS title page, not reconstructed from a metadata aggregator.

Where the lead is exposed is not citation fidelity but the depth of the citation chain beneath Ondrejcin's own accelerator sentence: it rests on a 1955 handbook cite with no measurement, and the modern pyrohydrolysis-accelerator literature names WO3, U3O8 and Cr2O3 while treating alumina as the matrix, never the catalyst. The bridge also quietly converts "composition of a reactively-grown corrosion product" into "Al2O3 activity in the protective scale", which are different physical objects.

The one upstream verifier who voted refuted (score 3) argued: CR-1133 Table III reports a Ti-8Al-1Mo-1V hot-salt crack threshold temperature of 232 C — on the alloy with the 28.6 wt% Al corrosion product, i.e. the maximum-scale-alumina case in the 1962-72 matrix. A ~4x spread in scale Al across that series moved severity and threshold stress, never onset temperature; lower-Al Ti-6Al-4V has a reported field failure at 220 C (wrong sign); and at 300-500 C a TiAl scale is a thin transient mixed TiO2/Al2O3 film, not protective alpha-alumina — so interfacial alumina activity may be far lower than the bulk composition suggests. The two paper + bench steps above are designed to settle the catalytic-rationale half directly before anyone commits to an SCC rig.

Caveats carried from the upstream verifiers:

- The Cranfield PhD thesis "Elevated Temperature Oxidation and Corrosion of a Titanium Aluminide Alloy" (handle 1826/3584) is the closest grey-literature threat. Indirect reporting indicates air/SO2 kinetics at or above 800 C (not NaCl, not stressed, not the lead's window), but the document itself could not be opened (HTTP 403) and should be pulled from the Cranfield repository before anyone commits to the stress ladder.
- Yao & Marek (1995) found no NaCl-induced corrosion of TiAl in oxygen-free exposure, so the route needs oxygen/moisture — consistent with pyrohydrolysis.
- Dye's group argues oxygen (not hydrogen) from water vapour is the main embrittling species at 450 C, which would partly decouple cracking severity from the HCl source term this lead keys on.
- ONERA's US 8,617,323 B2 already treats salt-exposed TiAl parts as needing protection and describes an AlCl3 → HCl → re-attack loop; the chemistry is not virgin ground even if the stressed threshold question is.
