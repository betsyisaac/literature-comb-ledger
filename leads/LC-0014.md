headline: In 1915 Curtis showed that sunlight-aged ebonite (hard rubber, roughly 30 wt% sulfur) loses its low-humidity surface-resistivity plateau because light plus air convert sulfur to hygroscopic sulfate films that a water soak removes. Inverse-vulcanised polymers (a 2010s class of sulfur-rich polymers made by reacting elemental S with olefins) have no established ageing readout for the same chemistry. The lead proposes running Curtis's humidity sweep and water-soak on UV-exposed IV plaques, paired with ion chromatography for sulfate — a cheap screen the modern IV-ageing literature has not applied.

modern_problem: Inverse vulcanisation (IV) polymers — made from elemental sulfur plus olefins such as DCPD, DIB or triglycerides — have been studied for a decade without a standard ageing protocol. The closest modern paper is Schmitt et al. 2026 (RSC Appl. Polym., d6lp00159a), which reports IV photo-ageing but, per an earlier full-text read by the upstream project, does not measure sulfate, surface resistivity, or XPS. Eight+ searches pairing inverse vulcanisation with sulfate / sulfuric acid / photo-oxidation / surface resistivity / humidity / ebonite / hard rubber (plus OpenAlex) returned nothing linking the two literatures.
cheapest_check: About 2-4 weeks and under USD 300-500 in consumables, given access to an fA-class electrometer. Press 50 wt% S p(S-DCPD) or p(S-DIB) plaques (at least 3 per arm); expose one arm to UV-A (or a south window) and keep a dark twin for 2-4 weeks; measure guarded surface resistivity (ASTM D257) over saturated-salt RH jars at roughly 11 / 33 / 53 / 75 / 94% plus P2O5-dry; rinse with 5 mL DI water and record conductivity, pH, and IC sulfate vs carboxylates; soak 48 h and repeat the RH sweep. Include a sulfur-free polymer control. Pass = exposed plaques lose the low-RH plateau, water-wash restores it, and the ionic load is predominantly SO4(2-). Read the Schmitt 2026 SI "Oxidation" section (currently 404) for S=O / sulfate bands before running.

Upstream entry dated 2026-11-12, scored 6/6/5 with 0 of 3 verifiers refuting. All three verifiers independently read Curtis 1915 and confirmed its claims verbatim: Fig. 10 showing new hard rubber "changes but little" from 0-50% RH while sunlight-exposed rubber keeps changing down to the lowest humidity (about 10^11 change in resistivity between P2O5-dry and 95% RH); Curtis's attribution to light-plus-air oxidation of sulfur to "the equivalent of sulfuric acid"; the January-1907 south-window sample that was essentially dead by July 1908 while its dark-stored twin "never showed marked changes"; a 2-day distilled-water soak as the only effective recovery ("The essential factor in improving the insulation is water"); quartz-mercury-lamp accelerated ageing (19-20 h ≈ months of sunlight); and a built-in sulfur-free control (Electrose) that showed "no formation of salts on the surface." Curtis himself calls the sunlight results "qualitative rather than quantitative."

Caveats carried from the verifiers, in strict form:

1. **The readout is not specific on its own.** Loss of the low-RH plateau plus wash-recovery shows only that some water-soluble hygroscopic ionic film has formed. IV polymers are about 50 wt% hydrocarbon, and their photo-oxidation yields carboxylic acids; oil-based IV also releases ester-hydrolysis products. The protocol only discriminates with ion chromatography for sulfate vs formate/acetate on the rinse, run alongside a sulfur-free polymer control. The lead is a screening readout, not a decisive test.

2. **Curtis's accelerated arm does not transfer.** His fast ageing used an unfiltered quartz-mercury lamp (UVC/UVB-rich), not the ~350 nm UV-A used in modern IV photoreactors; the apparent speed does not carry over.

3. **The chemistries differ.** Ebonite is 30-40 wt% S, mostly as cyclic mono/disulfide crosslinks in a natural-rubber matrix with fillers. IV polymers have photolabile polysulfide linkages plus blooming free S8. The hypothesis is that the end-state (surface sulfate film under light + moisture) overlaps despite the different starting chemistry.

4. **It cannot settle why outdoor ageing exceeds UV-A ageing.** Whorlow 1952 (abstract only; see caveat 7) names surface temperature during exposure as the dominant variable.

5. **"Near-zero cost" is overstated.** Guarded measurement at 10^16-10^18 ohm needs an fA-class electrometer and ring electrode. Most polymer-ageing labs do not have one on the bench.

6. **The parent target already lists guarded surface resistivity.** What is new is the humidity sweep and the wash-recovery step.

7. **Unverified sources on the modern side.** Schmitt 2026 HTML / PDF returned 403 and its SI returned 404 this run; the project read the main text previously but has not read the SI, which a search snippet says contains an "Oxidation" section with differential ATR-FTIR that could include S=O bands. The historic RCT hard-rubber follow-ups (Church 1946, Fisher & Scott 1949, Whorlow 1952) remain unread in full — Allen Press returned 403 again. If Schmitt's SI already reports surface sulfate measurements, the novelty narrows to the specific RH-sweep + wash-recovery combination; the cheap check still stands but its result would be less surprising.
