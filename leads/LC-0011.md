Caveats, carried as the upstream verifiers raised them:

1. **Banks himself excludes hermetic and pit storage from the sealed-storage engineering review in its first paragraph.** He drew the exact boundary this lead proposes to cross — which is both the undercut and the mechanism for the gap. The <5 %/day number is scoped to 300-10,000 t storages; it is a method, not a number to carry across.

2. **The lead's stated rationale is partly wrong physics.** The breathing volume in a filled bag is interstitial void air (~25-30 L in a 50 kg bag at 40% porosity), not headspace, and a thin bag tracks nearly the full diurnal swing while a 10,000 t bulk is thermally buffered. Thermal fractional interchange (dT/T ~ 5% per cycle) does not "almost vanish" at bag scale and may exceed the silo case. The surviving part of the claim is the area/volume term (~75-100x bag-vs-silo), which does hold. The experimental design above (indoor/outdoor contrast, two fill levels) is intended to measure the thermal term rather than argue around it.

3. **Envelope compliance can confound pressure-decay on a floppy liner.** A floppy liner is compliant, so a 250 Pa decay half-life on a 50 kg bag conflates compliance with leakage; Banks tested rigid filled bins. The two-fill-level regression above separates these.

4. **The actual derivation lives in CSIRO Technical Paper No. 13 (1977), 23 pp, which no verifier obtained.** The scaling argument rests on an unread paper; ILL request is the first move.

5. **Pre-emption is partial.** Carpaneto et al. 2016 already apply pressure-decay testing to field plastic grain bags; the novelty here reduces to a size extension plus a biologically calibrated threshold.

6. **Vocabulary split is not as clean as first framed.** The packaging world already owns whole-package leak tests (ASTM F2095 pressure decay with restraining plates, F1140, F2338), including documented handling of film-stretch and volume-sensitivity confounds. The ISU NC-213 model couples OTR to a whole-bag O2 balance.

7. **The upstream refuting verifier (score 2)** argued the Banks criterion is used with the wrong sign (Banks's optimum marks where extra sealing is wasted; the hermetic-bag field already tolerates leaky containers because insect respiration dominates O2 depletion), and that the 2-5 % O2 target is not scale-free (lethality runs through desiccation and depends on infestation, temperature and moisture; 8% gives no complete mortality per Ranabhat/Maier Insects 2023). Any proposal should quote <2 % as the biologically meaningful threshold rather than 2-5 %.
