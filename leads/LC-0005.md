headline: Calcium phosphate (apatite / amorphous calcium phosphate) has apparently never been tested as an atmospheric ice-nucleating phase, although the insect-cryobiology literature names insoluble calcium-phosphate crystalloid deposits as a distinct class of endogenous ice nucleators; apatite is a real accessory component of soil and desert dust, bone and guano dust, and phosphate-fertilizer and phosphogypsum dust.
old_source: Lee RE, Costanzo JP, Mugnano JA (1996), "Regulation of supercooling and ice nucleation in insects", Eur J Entomol 93(3):405-418. https://www.eje.cz/artkey/eje-199603-0011_Regulation_of_supercooling_and_ice_nucleation_in_insects.php — abstract and body state that insoluble crystalloid deposits such as calcium phosphate, present in a variety of insects, represent a newly discovered class of endogenous ice nucleators. Primary measurement: Mugnano, Lee & Taylor (1996), calcium-phosphate spherules raised crystallization T in 10 uL droplets from -18.4 to -10.1 C.
modern_problem: Kaufmann et al. (2016), "Ice nucleation efficiency of natural dust samples in the immersion mode", Atmos. Chem. Phys. 16:11177 — reference-mineral immersion-freezing survey; mineral list is quartz, calcite, clays, K-feldspars, (Na,Ca)-feldspars, microcline, dolomite, muscovite, dolostone and Arizona test dust; no phosphate of any kind. Four further searches returned zero studies measuring the ice-nucleating ability of any phosphate mineral. A 2026 ACP perthite-feldspar paper notes small apatite inclusions in a tested feldspar sample and does not test them.
cheapest_check: Standard microlitre droplet-freezing cold-stage assay (FINC / NIPI / Micro-PINGUIN style, 96 x 1 uL) on synthetic hydroxyapatite, synthetic fluorapatite and a geological standard such as Durango apatite — each ball-milled and BET-characterised — at 0.1 and 1.0 wt%, with microcline as positive control and milled quartz as negative. Report n_s(T). Roughly USD 1-3k and 2-3 days of bench time for any group that already owns a cold stage. A null result kills the lead immediately.

Caveats, all preserved from the source ledger:

1. **Partial prior art, and it is real.** Cornwell et al. 2024 (JGR-Atmospheres, SGP site) found PO2- and PO3- signals significantly enriched in ice-crystal residuals versus ambient particles by single-particle mass spectrometry. The atmospheric phosphate-INP link is therefore not untouched; what is untouched is the laboratory measurement.
2. **The numbers are weak.** The primary Mugnano/Lee/Taylor measurement is in 10 uL droplets, raising crystallization T from -18.4 to -10.1 C. Rescaled to micron particles in picolitre cloud droplets, that warm-onset claim weakens considerably. One verifier marked the lead refuted on this basis.
3. **Abundance.** Apatite is typically well under 1 wt% of soil and desert dust, so even microcline-like per-area activity would make a minor global contribution. The bridge is strongest for localised sources (fertilizer, phosphogypsum, bone and guano dust), not the global INP reservoir.
4. **Relevance is partly self-undercutting.** The scout framed this against the Southern Ocean shortwave bias, but the Southern Ocean is the most dust-poor region on Earth. A new continental-dust accessory mineral speaks to the Arctic / continental half of the problem at best.
5. The 1996 source gives no nucleation temperature, no surface-area normalisation, and no proof that the deposit rather than an associated protein is the active phase.

What survives the caveats is a cheap, falsifiable laboratory measurement that nobody has made on reference apatite samples. If positive, the natural home is addition to community INP parameterization datasets; if null, the lead dies for the cost of a few days of cold-stage time.
