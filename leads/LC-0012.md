modern_problem: The modern "fluorine effect" / halogen-effect literature on gamma-TiAl (HF dip, F implantation, polymer spray) is validated only for unstressed oxidation at 700-1050 C with mass-gain / scale-constitution endpoints; the fluorine-effect review (IntechOpen ch. 61032) does not mention NaCl, sea salt, hot-salt corrosion, SCC or hydrogen embrittlement, and its thermodynamics run 700-1100 C with the lowest actual test at 900 C. Patented in service for turbocharger rotors. Nothing in the modern corpus tests 300-500 C because below ~600 C oxidation is not the design concern — so the regime where HSSCC happens is precisely the regime the oxidation community has no reason to visit.

Upstream entry dated 2026-10-26, scored 7/6/2 (one of three verifiers refuted). The lead's own stated label is "sign test," not mitigation proposal — fitting, because the primary source itself contains the strongest counter-evidence (the H2-gas section where F-pretreated Ti-5Al-2.5Sn absorbed more hydrogen than blanks and fractured more severely), which the scout surfaced rather than buried.

The two surviving verifiers (scores 7 and 6) independently fetched DP(NASA)-1179, OCR'd it, and confirmed every number, quote, author line and the self-undercut verbatim. The novelty disjunction also reproduced on independent search: fluorine-effect/halogen-effect literature is unstressed oxidation at 700-1100 C; hot-salt SCC literature is conventional Ti alloys at 300-500 C stressed; no source tests fluorine-treated TiAl under chloride.

The refuting verifier (score 2) raised a substantive mechanism objection worth carrying forward unmodified: the modern review the lead itself cites reports p(TiF4,g) peaking near 8.4 bar at 400 C (dead centre of the proposed window) and names AlF — not AlF3 — as the Al carrier, with AlF2/AlF3 "explicitly inert" in the mechanism, so both legs of the proposed inversion may run backwards. The verifier also notes the predicted outcome (F-pretreated Ti-5Al-2.5Sn absorbing more H2 and fracturing more severely) is already in the same 1969 report on a LOW-Al alloy, attributed to oxide thinning with no Al dependence — so the one-alloy check as framed may not discriminate the new hypothesis from the old explanation.

Other carried caveats: the central step is a thermochemical inference across a 3-5x change in Al content with no 1962-72 datum behind it; the 1969 cracking and H2-gas experiments differ in hydrogen source, salt presence and stress state at once, so the sign inversion is consistent with the data rather than demonstrated by it; a patented in-service treatment carries residual risk a licensee has run salt testing without publishing (which the DECHEMA email is designed to close for free).
