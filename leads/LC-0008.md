The upstream verdict is "worth one cheap check, with the framing narrowed first." Three important corrections from the verifiers must travel with any writeup:

1. **The source is NOT a continuous 1906-1940 series** as any over-eager framing might imply. The bulletin states "only those made at seeding time are considered here," and no 4-digit year later than 1934 appears in the text. It is an annual autumn snapshot covering only the drought's onset.

2. **"The only such numbers in print" is false.** Rogler & Haas 1947 (Agron. J. 39(5), Mandan ND, fall moisture to 3 and 6 ft, station record from 1915) covers 1935-1940, which SB273 lacks. Cole & Mathews USDA Circular 563 (1940) prints similar foot-section available-water determinations for 15 Great Plains stations 1907-1938. SSSAJ 18:247-252 (1954) publishes the USDA Dry-Land programme's soil-moisture data for 31 Great Plains locations and includes the per-depth field-capacity and minimum-point calibration that SB273 declines to give. Any publishable work should use these as the broader denominator, not SB273 alone.

3. **Plot-scale fallow records are not landscape.** Fallow storage efficiency is itself drought-dependent; the bulletin's own fallow-water-to-yield correlation is "a very low degree of relationship" (r = 0.271). Grid-cell comparison must treat printed plots as a bracket on the range, not a point estimate.
